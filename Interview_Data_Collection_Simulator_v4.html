Interview before you solve

Your goal is not to make the stakeholder approve an idea. Your goal is to understand what the person actually did, expected, noticed, and struggled with.

Five habits of a strong interviewer

1. Ask about a real event

Past behavior usually provides better evidence than predicted behavior.

Better: Tell me about the last time this happened.

Use carefully: Would you use this every week?

2. Ask for the sequence

Walkthroughs expose actions, transitions, and points of confusion.

Try: What happened next? What were you thinking then?

3. Follow the user's words

If the person mentions a delay, workaround, another person, or hesitation, explore it instead of returning to a fixed script.

Try: You mentioned the long gap. What made it difficult?

4. Look for exceptions

A time when the problem did not occur can reveal conditions that support success.

Try: Tell me about a time this worked well. What was different?

5. Stay neutral

Leading and solution-first questions can produce polite agreement instead of reliable evidence.

Avoid: Would a bigger sign fix this?

Better: What did you notice first?
